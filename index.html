EXTERNAL BALLISTICS CALCULATOR

Developed by a Czech-Swedish hunter and handloader, this tool is designed for practical hunting and handloading scenarios, with a focus on real-world implications rather than purely theoretical values.

A central objective is clarity and understanding. The three-phase model separates the rifle's mechanical zero, the contribution of atmospheric conditions, and propellant temperature — making their individual contributions explicit. The aim is to support intuition and informed judgment, not merely to produce numerical output.

On the standard test load, trajectory predictions agree with JBM Ballistics within JBM's 0.1 in rounding out to 500 m, and velocity within 0.1 m/s; a QuickTARGET comparison agrees within about 1 mm and 1 m/s to 300 m.

This tool was developed with the assistance of Claude by Anthropic.

⚠ Disclaimer
This calculator is provided for informational and educational purposes only. All ballistic calculations are theoretical models and may differ from real-world results due to ammunition variation, atmospheric conditions, barrel characteristics, and other factors.

Never rely solely on software for safe firearm handling. Always follow applicable firearms laws and hunting regulations. The author accepts no liability for any use of this tool.

Model & Mathematics ▶ expand
Trajectory Model
Point-mass ODE integrated with RK4 (dt = 1 ms):
dvx/dt = −g·sin(α) − a_drag · (vx/v)
dvy/dt = −g·cos(α) − a_drag · (vy/v)
(x along the line of sight, y perpendicular to it, α = shooting angle)

where:
a_drag = (π/8) · ρ · v² · Cd(M) / BCSI
π/8 = 0.3927  (Siacci/Ingalls normalization, matches Gehtsoft G1 table)
BCSI = BCG1 × 703.07  [kg/m²]
ρ = (P_d · M_d + e · M_w) / (R · T)  [kg/m³]  (humid air, M_d=28.964, M_w=18.016 g/mol)
P = actual pressure on site; from a sea-level value P₀: P = P₀ · (1 − L·h/T₀)n, T₀ = T + L·h, L = 0.0065 K/m, n = g·M_d/(R·L) = 5.2557
vs = 331.3 · √(T/273.15) · √((γmix/γd) · (M_d/Mmix))  [m/s]  (humid air, exact ideal-gas mixture)
x = e/P,  Mmix = (1−x)·M_d + x·M_w,  γ = cp/(cp−R) with cp/R = (1−x)·3.5 + x·4.04
e = RH/100 · e_s,  e_s = 6.1078 · 10^(7.5·T_C/(237.3+T_C))  [hPa] (Tetens/Murray formula)
M = v / vs  (Mach number)

G1/G7 drag function Cd(M): Gehtsoft G1 tabulation (79 Mach points, 0–5), π/8 normalization (Siacci/Ingalls convention).
Zero angle θ: found by bisection over ±0.30 rad (stops at ε < 10⁻¹⁰ rad, about 33 iterations) for a level shot in the zero-day atmosphere.
Wind drift: lag-time method — z = w · (tflight − x/v₀), evaluated on the shooting-day trajectory.
Spin effects (optional, Litz): spin drift = 1.25·(Sg+1.2)·TOF1.83 [in], shown relative to the line of sight (zero at the zero range); aerodynamic jump = 0.01·Sg − 0.0024·L + 0.032 [MOA per mph crosswind], with L = 4.2 calibers as a typical bullet length. Empirical estimates, reported as separate columns.
Shooting angle α: zero solved level, shot computed with the full gravity vector (g·cos α across, g·sin α along the line of sight). Both uphill and downhill hit higher than level.
Three-Phase Model
Phase 1 — Zero: bisection solves for θ under zero-day atmosphere (Tzero, Pzero, RHzero). θ is fixed thereafter — it represents the rifle's mechanical setting.
Phase 2 — Shooting day: same θ, new atmosphere (Tshoot, Pshoot, RHshoot). Shows the pure external ballistic effect of weather change — typically a few mm at ≤200 m.
Phase 3 — Propellant temperature EXPERIMENTAL: same θ and shooting atmosphere, but v₀ is shifted by the propellant temperature effect: Δv₀ = k · (Tshoot − Tzero), where k is the user-measured sensitivity [m/s/°C] and propellant temperature is assumed equal to shooting temperature. This is the dominant real-world weather effect — a −30°C temperature swing with a sensitive powder (k = 0.5 m/s/°C) shifts v₀ by 15 m/s. Disabled when k = 0.

Model Notes
▸ Trajectory and velocity agree with JBM — Gehtsoft G1 drag tabulation with π/8 (Siacci/Ingalls) normalization. No empirical correction factors.
▸ Point-mass model (2D) — drop and velocity agree with JBM within its rounding to 500 m on the test load.
▸ Relative humidity modelled — Tetens/Murray saturation pressure, partial-pressure density and humid-air speed of sound. Effect <0.5 mm at ≤200 m.
▸ G1 and G7 drag models supported — G7 recommended for VLD/boattail bullets.
▸ Spin drift and aerodynamic jump optional (Litz); no Coriolis — Coriolis is negligible at hunting ranges.
▸ Steep shots — crosswind on slopes uses the lag-time rule (about ±18 mm at 600 m and ±70 mm at 1000 m on a 45° slope), and air density and speed of sound are held at the muzzle values (about +15 mm at 600 m on a 45° slope).
▸ Pressure reference per day — a sea-level value (as from weather apps) is converted to the actual pressure on site with the entered elevation and that day's temperature; a pressure on site is used as entered.
▸ Phase 3 (propellant temperature) is experimental — user-measured v₀ sensitivity [m/s/°C]. Disabled when k = 0.
▸ Velocity, energy and wind columns are shooting-day values (incl. Phase 3); the hunt class uses E₁₀₀ in the ICAO standard atmosphere.
